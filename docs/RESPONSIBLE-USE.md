# Responsible use: automate the work, not the sameness

channel-autopilot makes it easy to publish every day. YouTube, Instagram and Facebook reward channels that are useful
and punish channels that are not. These rules protect your channels as much as your viewers.

## YouTube's rules that matter most here

- **Inauthentic content** (YouTube Partner Program): content that is mass-produced or repetitive, made from templates with
  little variation, or easily replicable at scale is not eligible for monetisation, and channels built on it are removed.
  Each channel needs its own clear purpose, voice and look; each video needs its own substance.
- **Disclosure of altered or synthetic content**: when a video shows realistic people, places or events that did not
  happen, set the "altered content" disclosure in YouTube Studio. Check YouTube's current rules for synthetic voices and
  imagery on your channel.
- **Spam and deceptive practices**: titles, thumbnails and descriptions must match the video. No fake urgency, no
  promises the video does not keep.
- **API Services terms**: one Cloud project per application; do not create projects to get around quotas.

## Defaults this project keeps, and why you should too

- **Pace**: the rotation's default is 3 videos a day across all your channels. More is not better.
- **Facts from sources**: the example writer prompt requires every number to come from a source opened that day, listed in
  the description. A wrong number in a money, health or law video can hurt someone.
- **Disclaimers**: money, law and health topics say they are for education, not advice.
- **No other people's assets**: no stock footage you have not licensed, no logos or app screens you do not own, no
  imitation of another creator's characters, voice or format.
- **A human in charge**: look at the first videos of every new channel or format before you schedule it, read the daily
  report, and use the dashboard's pause switch when something looks off.

## Where the keys come from

You bring your own Google, Meta and AWS keys; the project ships none and never sends your keys anywhere except the
platforms' own APIs and your own AWS account.
